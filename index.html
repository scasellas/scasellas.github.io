Case study · GoodLoan · Oct 2026

Counting every call

GoodLoan now counts every call and outgoing text each sales agent makes on their company iPhone, per day and per hour, with no app on the phone and nothing for the agent to do.

1,468calls counted in the first 7 business days
86%of outgoing calls connected
18.1talk hours across 4 agents
0apps installed, taps required

The problem

Managers had no count of how many calls agents made. Agents dial borrowers from the native Phone app on company iPhones, and that activity left no record anyone could report on.

  • iOS walls off the call log. No App Store app and no device-management tool can read call history on an iPhone.
  • No calling platform to pull from. Calls go over the cellular line, not a dialer with its own logs.
  • The carrier has no API. Call logs are a manual download, line by line.

What didn't work

Six routes were ruled out first, each by testing on a real company phone.

ApproachWhy it failed
iCloud call-history sync to a MacManaged Apple Accounts quietly disable it, and re-enabling needs newer macOS than most office Macs can run.
Device-management queryiOS exposes no call log to MDM.
Carrier call recordsNo API, manual download only. Kept as a weekly cross-check.
Unencrypted iPhone backupThe call database is silently left out.
Encrypted backup as configurediOS asked for confirmation on the phone for every run, so nothing could run unattended.
Re-enrolling every phoneMeans erasing each phone, with no guarantee it fixes the prompt. Not attempted.

The solution

A small service on the office server takes an encrypted backup of each phone over Wi-Fi, keeps only the call-history and message databases, and turns them into per-agent daily counts. Decrypted files exist only in a temporary folder while they are counted, then they're deleted. Because a backup carries the phone's existing call log, counts reached back a week on the first day.

What it tracks

Eleven metrics per agent per day, all from metadata: no call audio, no message text, and the other party's number is never stored or shown. Calls, outgoing and connected, incoming, answered and missed, talk hours, unique numbers dialed, calls by hour of day, and texts out split by SMS and iMessage. A dashboard shows them as KPI cards, a trend line, a per-agent calendar heatmap and an hour-of-day chart, refreshed hourly. IT gets a Slack alert when a phone hasn't backed up in a day, and agents are told in writing that call activity is monitored.

Results

Over the first seven business days the team averaged 210 calls a day, with the busiest day at 298. Calling runs from 9:00 to 18:00 with almost nothing outside it, so the hour-of-day view doubles as a check on working hours.

Limits, stated plainly

  • Calls outside the Phone app (WhatsApp, FaceTime, dialer apps) aren't counted.
  • A phone off the office Wi-Fi is caught up on its next sweep, so a day's total is final the next morning.
  • Each backup streams the whole phone even though two databases are kept: 4 to 16 minutes per phone.
  • Unattended backups came with a security tradeoff on the phones, accepted deliberately and monitored, with a supervised-device route being priced to remove it.

Lessons

  • Check the account type first. Claiming a domain in Apple Business Manager makes every Apple ID managed, which quietly disables iCloud call-history sync.
  • Test the platform's limits on one device before building. Both blockers were found that way; neither is clearly documented.
  • Keep the data minimal. Two databases, decrypted only long enough to count. That made the monitoring easier to explain to the team.
  • Make each new metric best-effort. A failed texting read never breaks the daily call count.
